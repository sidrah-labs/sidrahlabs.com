Why us

The hard part isn't moving data. It's changing systems without breaking trust.

Legacy systems encode years of business rules, but those rules are rarely documented well enough to safely migrate or modify. Meanwhile, brittle pipelines and metered tools make change costly. We bring the engineering and verification infrastructure to modernize these systems without losing confidence in the results.

  1. 01

    Undocumented rules make modernization risky

    Legacy databases and ERPs hold critical business logic in schemas, queries, and operational habits. Without a reliable way to surface and check those rules, teams cannot tell whether a migration preserves the meaning of the data.

  2. 02

    Pipeline economics punish growth

    Per-row connectors can make routine data movement more expensive as volumes grow. For the right workloads, operating pipelines in your own cloud can reduce recurring costs and give you more control.

  3. 03

    Faster code needs stronger guarantees

    Basic tests catch syntax and known examples, not every semantic regression. As engineers and AI coding tools produce changes faster, teams need automated checks that establish whether critical behavior has been preserved.

Research

Building verification for software that changes at AI speed.

AI can generate code faster than teams can manually inspect every change. The challenge is not just producing software, but establishing that it preserves the properties people rely on.

We are developing verification tools that analyze data logic before execution. Using SMT solvers such as Z3 and relational-algebra analysis, we investigate whether proposed query and pipeline changes can violate specified rules. Our long-term goal is to extend this infrastructure to software generated automatically.

How this connects to client work: Modernization engagements expose real failure modes and business rules. Those lessons shape our tools, while advances in verification help make future deployments safer and more repeatable.
